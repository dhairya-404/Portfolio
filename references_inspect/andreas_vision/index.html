Writing

  • Where the cooldown went

    Agents took the bricklaying, and the bricklaying was where my head rested between decisions. Batching the thinking gives some of that rest back.

  • Why do the Mac's menus read like sentences?

    The first Mac named its menus with nouns and filled them with verbs, so every pick finishes a sentence. Forty-two years on the Finder still parses. Most product menus don't.

  • Why does Google Maps stop at the door?

    Even at Oxford Circus, Google Maps draws a Tube logo and a name. Naver draws every exit, the lifts and the change of line, and routes through them: the layer AR will need.

  • The hidden design system in album covers

    Line up a run of covers from one artist and the rules show. Sakepnk changes one shape per song, ziproom changes the place around the two of them, and both let you spot their records before you read a word.

  • What is a design engineer, anyway?

    One title, two job listings that want nearly opposite people. Here's how I read the role, where I sit on it, and why the mess makes this the best time to be in the field.

  • Why does Korea paint its roads pink?

    Korean road wayfinding and how it can decrease user load in split-second decision situations.

  • Why did the formatting toolbar jump when you pressed Bold?

    Bold makes text wider, and a toolbar centred on the selection slides with it. The obvious fix made it worse. The right one came from asking what never moves.
